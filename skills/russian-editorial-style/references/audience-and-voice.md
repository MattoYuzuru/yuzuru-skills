# Audience and voice

## Audience card

Before a material adaptation, infer or record:

- who will read it and in what setting;
- what they already know and what terminology they use;
- what they need to understand, decide, feel, or do;
- the cost of misunderstanding;
- expected relationship and form of address;
- channel, length, and accessibility constraints.

If information is missing, choose neutral contemporary Russian and avoid
assumptions about age, identity, profession, or values.

## Register dimensions

Translate vague requests such as “живее” into observable choices:

- formal ↔ conversational;
- distant ↔ directly addressed;
- compressed ↔ explanatory;
- neutral ↔ openly evaluative;
- restrained ↔ emotionally expressive;
- specialist ↔ newcomer-oriented;
- explicit transitions ↔ more implicit literary flow.

Change only the dimensions needed for the audience and genre. “Natural” does not
mean informal, emotional, irregular, or imprecise.

## Working from author samples

Prefer user-authored examples from the same genre. Extract tendencies rather
than phrases to copy:

- form of address and point of view;
- terminology and preferred level of explanation;
- sentence and paragraph texture without numerical quotas;
- humour, imagery, stance, and degree of qualification;
- typical openings, transitions, and endings when they are genuinely recurrent.

Preserve distinctive source wording unless it causes the identified problem.
Do not claim exact voice cloning from a few examples, invent biography, or make
every sentence display the same trait. When samples conflict, privilege the
sample closest to the current genre and state the uncertainty if it matters.

## Ownership check

The revision should still express the author's supplied position, not the
editor's preferred personality. Check that added confidence, humour, intimacy,
anger, or enthusiasm came from the brief or samples. Remove generic “personality”
that was added only to make prose appear human.
