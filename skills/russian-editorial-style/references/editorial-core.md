# Editorial core

## Diagnose by reader effect

Name what fails for the intended reader before changing style.

| Problem | Evidence in the text | Useful intervention |
|---|---|---|
| Main point is buried | Reader must cross background before learning why it matters | Move the decision, claim, or task forward |
| Actor or action is hidden | Abstract noun chains or passive wording obscure responsibility | Restore the actor and verb when responsibility matters |
| Referent is unclear | “Это”, “данный”, or a pronoun can point to several things | Name the intended object |
| Logic is implicit | Condition, contrast, cause, or chronology can be misread | State the relation with order or a precise connective |
| Concept is unfamiliar | Simpler words do not supply the missing premise | Define, exemplify, or explain the concept |
| Claim is too broad | Evidence supports a narrower population, period, or condition | Restore the supported scope and uncertainty |
| Terminology drifts | One object receives several near-synonyms | Choose the stable term and keep it |
| Passage is redundant | Removing it loses neither meaning nor navigation | Delete or combine it |

Do not diagnose a phrase in isolation. A passive, nominalization, repetition,
long sentence, list, or connective can be functional in context.

## Minimal-edit procedure

1. State the paragraph's job in a few words.
2. Mark the sentence that carries that job.
3. Remove material that neither supports the job nor provides necessary context.
4. Put prerequisites before dependent claims and conditions before actions.
5. Make actors, references, and logical relations explicit where ambiguity remains.
6. Edit sentences for precision and natural Russian only after the structure works.
7. Stop. A second stylistic pass needs a specific unresolved problem.

## Concrete and abstract language

Use available names, actions, objects, examples, and measurements when they help
the reader decide, imagine, or act. Never fabricate them. Keep abstraction when
it names the subject of analysis, a reusable concept, or a necessary technical
category. Explain an unfamiliar abstraction instead of swapping it for a vague
“simple” synonym.

## Cohesion

Use paragraph order, repeated key terms, pronouns with one clear antecedent, and
precise connectives to show relations. A connective is useful when it identifies
the relation more accurately than adjacency alone. Remove it only when it adds
ceremony without information.

## Preservation pass

Compare source and revision separately from the style review:

- names, numbers, dates, units, ranges, versions, and comparisons;
- links, citations, quotations, code, commands, and identifiers;
- who did what, to whom, when, and under which condition;
- negation, modality, causal direction, exceptions, and limitations;
- stable terminology and attributed positions;
- information deliberately omitted to meet a requested length limit.

Similarity is not proof of preservation. If the revision changes a protected
claim, restore it or ask the user to approve the substantive change.

## Audit format

Prioritize a short list. A material finding explains how a reader could
misunderstand, fail to act, or receive a different claim. Minor preferences
belong last and should not trigger a rewrite on their own.
