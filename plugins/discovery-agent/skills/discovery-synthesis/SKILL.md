# Discovery Synthesis

Read `references/decision-framework.md` when the evidence includes conflicting objectives,
commercial uncertainty, or a credible existing substitute.

## Workflow

1. Re-anchor on the original objective and subtle priorities.
2. Separate explicit needs, observed evidence, inferences, opportunities, and unresolved questions.
3. Group alternatives by the underlying job and identify why each succeeds or fails for this
   audience.
4. Prioritize opportunities by user value, evidence strength, feasibility, and differentiation.
5. Rank risks by likelihood, impact, detectability, and mitigation leverage.
6. Identify bottlenecks and decision conditions without inventing a full technical design.
7. Choose an honest recommendation and state what new evidence would change it.
8. Critique the synthesis for unsupported precision, missing regional context, and startup clichés.

## Output

Lead with the recommendation, strongest evidence, and decisive uncertainty. Keep the long tail of
features and risks out of the executive summary. Say explicitly when the business case is weak but
the personal, family, internal, open-source, or learning value is sound.

## Guardrails

- Existing competition proves neither demand nor futility.
- An open-source substitute may be the best answer; name integration or contribution as options.
- Do not upgrade `researched` to `validated` without real user or operational evidence.
- Preserve disagreements and unknowns instead of averaging them away.
